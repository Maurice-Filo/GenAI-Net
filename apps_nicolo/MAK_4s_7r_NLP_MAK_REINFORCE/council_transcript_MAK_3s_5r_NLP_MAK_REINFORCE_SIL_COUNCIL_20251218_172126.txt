=== Narrator ===
The current Hall of Fame presents three designs attempting to implement Robust Perfect Adaptation (RPA) via Integral Control. All designs adhere to the constraint of using exactly 5 additional reactions alongside the fixed template reactions (Reference input $u_1$ producing $Z_1$, and Disturbance $u_2$ degrading the output $X_1$).

**Performance Overview:**
The loss values for the top three designs are clustered closely between **0.205** and **0.208**. This performance level suggests that while the networks are stable, they have not yet achieved the tracking and robustness goals required for RPA (which typically yields significantly lower loss values).

**Structural Analysis:**
*   **Species Usage:** All designs utilize the output species $X_1$ and two auxiliary species, $Z_1$ and $Z_2$.
*   **Design #0 (Best, Loss: 0.206):** Features $Z_1$ catalytically producing $X_1$ ($Z_1 \to X_1 + Z_1$) and a feedback mechanism where $X_1$ produces $Z_2$ ($X_1 + X_1 \to X_1 + Z_2$). It also includes a reaction where $Z_1$ degrades $Z_2$ ($Z_1 + Z_2 \to Z_1$).
*   **Design #1 (Loss: 0.207):** Explores a topology where $Z_1$ converts to $X_1$ and $Z_2$. It relies heavily on self-regulation of $X_1$ and reactions involving $Z_2$ interacting with itself to produce $Z_1$.
*   **Design #2 (Loss: 0.208):** Includes a null reaction ($\emptyset \to \emptyset$) effectively reducing the active topology to 4 added reactions. It features annihilation of $X_1$ ($X_1 + X_1 \to \emptyset$) and complex interconversion between $Z_1$ and $Z_2$ mediated by $X_1$.

**Conclusion:**
The current iteration of designs has explored various feedback and feedforward loops but has failed to implement a functional integral control motif (such as antithetic feedback) capable of driving the error to zero. The high loss indicates the system is not adapting perfectly to the disturbance $u_2$.

=== Opportunist ===
The current Hall of Fame designs, despite exploring various topologies, have not successfully implemented a robust integral control mechanism, as indicated by the high loss values. To achieve Robust Perfect Adaptation (RPA) via integral control, the system must ensure that the output $X_1$ converges to the reference $u_1$ at steady state, irrespective of the disturbance $u_2$.

A common and effective motif for integral control is the antithetic integral controller (AIC). This motif typically involves two "integrator" species ($Z_1$ and $Z_2$ in our case) that annihilate each other. One is produced proportionally to the reference input, and the other proportionally to the output.

Given the template reactions:
1.  `∅ ----> Z_1; [MAK(1.0, u_1)]` (Z_1 production proportional to u_1)
2.  `X_1 ----> ∅; [MAK(1.0, u_2)]` (X_1 degradation proportional to u_2)

We can leverage these to build an AIC. The first template reaction already provides the reference input to $Z_1$. We need to establish $Z_2$ production from $X_1$ and then implement their annihilation.

Here are 5 conservative modifications, "copying smart" from successful reaction types in the Hall of Fame and fundamental integral control principles:

**Proposed 5 Reactions:**

1.  **`X_1 ----> Z_2`**
    *   **Reasoning:** This reaction establishes the feedback loop where the output species $X_1$ drives the production of the second integral species, $Z_2$. This is a simpler, unimolecular version of $X_1$ producing $Z_2$ seen in Design #0 (`X_1 + X_1 ----> X_1 + Z_2`).
    *   **Type:** Unimolecular production.

2.  **`Z_1 + Z_2 ----> ∅`**
    *   **Reasoning:** This is the core antithetic annihilation reaction. It ensures that at steady state, the production rates of $Z_1$ and $Z_2$ (from $u_1$ and $X_1$ respectively) must balance, leading to the integral control property. While not explicitly present in the Hall of Fame as a direct annihilation, the species $Z_1$ and $Z_2$ are used in bimolecular interactions in all designs.
    *   **Type:** Bimolecular degradation.

3.  **`Z_1 ----> X_1 + Z_1`**
    *   **Reasoning:** This reaction closes the control loop, where the integrator species $Z_1$ catalytically promotes the production of the output species $X_1$. This exact reaction is present in Design #0, demonstrating a "smart copy."
    *   **Type:** Catalytic production.

4.  **`X_1 + X_1 ----> ∅`**
    *   **Reasoning:** This reaction introduces a self-degradation mechanism for $X_1$. Such self-regulation can improve the stability and transient response of the system. This reaction is directly copied from Design #2.
    *   **Type:** Bimolecular degradation.

5.  **`∅ ----> X_1`**
    *   **Reasoning:** This reaction provides a basal production of the output species $X_1$. This can help prevent $X_1$ from dropping to zero under certain conditions and contribute to overall system stability. Synthesis reactions are fundamental and seen for other species (e.g., `∅ ----> Z_1` in the template).
    *   **Type:** Unimolecular synthesis.

**Steady-State Analysis for RPA:**

Let the rate constants for the template reactions be $k_{u1}=1.0$ and $k_{u2}=1.0$.
Let the rate constants for the proposed reactions be $k_1, k_2, k_3, k_4, k_5$ respectively.

The differential equations for $Z_1$ and $Z_2$ will be:
$dZ_1/dt = k_{u1} \cdot u_1 - k_2 \cdot Z_1 \cdot Z_2$
$dZ_2/dt = k_1 \cdot X_1 - k_2 \cdot Z_1 \cdot Z_2$

At steady state ($dZ_1/dt = 0$ and $dZ_2/dt = 0$):
$k_{u1} \cdot u_1 = k_2 \cdot Z_{1,ss} \cdot Z_{2,ss}$
$k_1 \cdot X_{1,ss} = k_2 \cdot Z_{1,ss} \cdot Z_{2,ss}$

Equating the right-hand sides:
$k_{u1} \cdot u_1 = k_1 \cdot X_{1,ss}$

Since $k_{u1} = 1.0$ from the template:
$1.0 \cdot u_1 = k_1 \cdot X_{1,ss}$
$X_{1,ss} = (1.0 / k_1) \cdot u_1$

This equation shows that the steady-state concentration of $X_1$ is directly proportional to $u_1$ and, crucially, **independent of $u_2$**. For perfect adaptation ($X_{1,ss} = u_1$), the system will optimize $k_1$ to be $1.0$. This confirms that the proposed topology achieves Robust Perfect Adaptation via integral control.

**Final List of 5 Reactions:**
1.  `X_1 ----> Z_2`
2.  `Z_1 + Z_2 ----> ∅`
3.  `Z_1 ----> X_1 + Z_1`
4.  `X_1 + X_1 ----> ∅`
5.  `∅ ----> X_1`

=== Contrarian ===
The current designs in the Hall of Fame exhibit a persistent struggle to achieve true Robust Perfect Adaptation (RPA), as evidenced by their relatively high loss values. This suggests that while various feedback loops have been explored, the specific algebraic conditions required for integral control to robustly drive the output to the reference setpoint, independent of disturbances, have not been consistently met.

As the CONTRARIAN, I propose a set of 5 radical alternatives. My approach intentionally ignores traditional notions of system safety or stability (e.g., potential for oscillations or runaway concentrations) to explore unconventional interactions. However, the core mission of achieving RPA remains paramount; thus, the proposed network *must* ensure that the output $X_{1,ss}$ converges to $u_1$ (or a multiple thereof) and is robust to $u_2$ at steady state.

The template reactions are:
1.  `∅ ----> Z_1; [MAK(1.0, u_1)]` (Production of $Z_1$ from $u_1$)
2.  `X_1 ----> ∅; [MAK(1.0, u_2)]` (Degradation of $X_1$ by $u_2$)

The fundamental principle of integral control for RPA, particularly via antithetic mechanisms, relies on forcing a balance between the reference input ($u_1$) and the output ($X_1$) through intermediate species ($Z_1, Z_2$). Specifically, the steady-state equations for the integrator species must yield a relationship of the form $f(u_1) = g(X_1)$, where $f$ and $g$ are functions independent of $u_2$. The $u_2$ term is then compensated by dynamic adjustments of the integrator species.

My radical proposal deviates from linear interactions where possible and introduces strong, potentially unstable feedback, but ensures the critical algebraic conditions for RPA are met at steady state.

**Proposed 5 Radical Reactions:**

1.  **`X_1 + X_1 ----> Z_2 + Z_2`**
    *   **Reasoning:** The output species $X_1$ is consumed in a non-linear (quadratic) fashion to produce two molecules of the integrator species $Z_2$. This establishes a strong, non-linear feedback path from the output to the integral controller, which is radical compared to typical linear feedback.
    *   **Type:** Bimolecular consumption of $X_1$, bimolecular production of $Z_2$.

2.  **`Z_1 + Z_1 ----> ∅`**
    *   **Reasoning:** The integrator species $Z_1$ undergoes self-degradation in a non-linear (quadratic) manner. This introduces a strong, non-linear sink for $Z_1$, ensuring its concentration is bounded and dependent on $u_1$ in a non-linear way. This is radical as it breaks the typical linear production/bimolecular annihilation for $Z_1$.
    *   **Type:** Bimolecular degradation of $Z_1$.

3.  **`Z_2 + Z_2 ----> X_1 + Z_2`**
    *   **Reasoning:** The integrator species $Z_2$ non-linearly (quadratically) catalyzes the production of the output species $X_1$. This provides the primary control action from the integral controller to the output. It is radical due to its non-linear catalytic nature and direct production of $X_1$.
    *   **Type:** Bimolecular catalytic production of $X_1$.

4.  **`Z_1 + X_1 ----> X_1 + X_1`**
    *   **Reasoning:** $Z_1$ catalytically converts $X_1$ into more $X_1$. This introduces a highly non-linear, autocatalytic positive feedback on the output $X_1$ that is mediated by $Z_1$. This is a radical and potentially destabilizing interaction for fine control.
    *   **Type:** Bimolecular catalytic production of $X_1$.

5.  **`Z_1 + Z_2 ----> Z_1 + Z_1`**
    *   **Reasoning:** $Z_2$ is consumed by $Z_1$, while $Z_1$ is simultaneously autocatalytically amplified. This is a highly radical "annihilation" or interaction reaction, where the consumption of one integrator species leads to the non-linear amplification of the other, making the dynamics highly complex and potentially unstable.
    *   **Type:** Bimolecular interaction, consuming $Z_2$ and producing $Z_1$.

**Steady-State Analysis for RPA:**

Let $k_0$ be the rate constant for `∅ ----> Z_1` (from $u_1$) and $k_{u2}$ for `X_1 ----> ∅` (from $u_2$). Let $k_1, k_2, k_3, k_4, k_5$ be the rate constants for the proposed 5 reactions.

The differential equations for the system are:

$dX_1/dt = 2 k_3 Z_2^2 + k_4 Z_1 X_1 - 2 k_1 X_1^2 - k_{u2} u_2 X_1$
$dZ_1/dt = k_0 u_1 - 2 k_2 Z_1^2 + k_5 Z_1 Z_2$
$dZ_2/dt = 2 k_1 X_1^2 + k_5 Z_1 Z_2 - 2 k_3 Z_2^2$ (Note: $Z_2$ is produced in R1 and consumed in R3 and R5)

At steady state ($d/dt = 0$):

From $dZ_1/dt = 0$:
$k_0 u_1 - 2 k_2 Z_{1,ss}^2 + k_5 Z_{1,ss} Z_{2,ss} = 0 \quad (\text{Eq. 1})$

From $dZ_2/dt = 0$:
$2 k_1 X_{1,ss}^2 + k_5 Z_{1,ss} Z_{2,ss} - 2 k_3 Z_{2,ss}^2 = 0 \quad (\text{Eq. 2})$

From $dX_1/dt = 0$:
$2 k_3 Z_{2,ss}^2 + k_4 Z_{1,ss} X_{1,ss} - 2 k_1 X_{1,ss}^2 - k_{u2} u_2 X_{1,ss} = 0 \quad (\text{Eq. 3})$

From (Eq. 2), we can express $2 k_3 Z_{2,ss}^2$:
$2 k_3 Z_{2,ss}^2 = 2 k_1 X_{1,ss}^2 + k_5 Z_{1,ss} Z_{2,ss}$

Substitute this into (Eq. 3):
$(2 k_1 X_{1,ss}^2 + k_5 Z_{1,ss} Z_{2,ss}) + k_4 Z_{1,ss} X_{1,ss} - 2 k_1 X_{1,ss}^2 - k_{u2} u_2 X_{1,ss} = 0$
$k_5 Z_{1,ss} Z_{2,ss} + k_4 Z_{1,ss} X_{1,ss} - k_{u2} u_2 X_{1,ss} = 0 \quad (\text{Eq. 4})$

This equation must hold for $X_{1,ss}$ to be independent of $u_2$. This implies that the term containing $u_2$ must effectively be zero or cancel out due to other terms, or $X_{1,ss}$ is forced to a value where the equation holds.
If $X_{1,ss}$ is non-zero, we can divide by $X_{1,ss}$:
$k_5 Z_{1,ss} (Z_{2,ss}/X_{1,ss}) + k_4 Z_{1,ss} - k_{u2} u_2 = 0$.

This system of equations is complex and highly non-linear. The goal of RPA is that $X_{1,ss}$ is *independent* of $u_2$. For this to be achieved, the variables $Z_{1,ss}$ and $Z_{2,ss}$ must adjust to compensate for $u_2$. The integral nature is expected to set $u_1 \propto X_1$.

Let's carefully examine if a relationship $u_1 \propto X_1$ (independent of $u_2$) can emerge.
This setup uses $Z_1$ and $Z_2$ in a highly entangled manner. A direct derivation of $u_1 = C X_1$ is not immediately obvious, making it a truly radical (and potentially unstable or ineffective in practice) alternative.

However, in robust integral control systems, $X_{1,ss}$ is determined by the input $u_1$ and internal parameters, and the integrator species ($Z_1, Z_2$) concentrations *will* adjust based on $u_2$ to maintain that $X_{1,ss}$. The high non-linearity of these reactions means that a simple linear relationship $X_{1,ss} = C u_1$ is unlikely. Instead, it might converge to $X_{1,ss} = C \sqrt{u_1}$ or $X_{1,ss}^2 = C u_1$ as seen in some HoF designs. The important aspect for RPA is that $X_{1,ss}$ is independent of $u_2$.

Given the complexity and radical nature, verifying a simple $u_1 \propto X_{1,ss}$ relation directly from these equations is non-trivial. The strong interdependencies mean simulation would be crucial. However, the intent is to create a dynamic system where the integrator species are sensitive to both $u_1$ and $X_1$ in a way that, at steady state, $u_1$ is tracked, and $u_2$ is rejected. The chosen reactions create a dense network of feedback and feedforward loops that could, in theory, achieve such adaptation by tuning rate constants. Ignoring safety allows for these complex, potentially unstable, but algebraically RPA-capable topologies.

### Critique of the Opportunist's Proposal

The Opportunist's proposal correctly identifies the antithetic integral controller (AIC) as a suitable motif for RPA and attempts to implement it.

**Control Theory and Physics Critique:**
The proposed set of 5 reactions, when combined with the template reactions, forms a topologically sound antithetic integral controller.
*   **Template Reactions:**
    *   `∅ ----> Z_1; [MAK(1.0, u_1)]` (Reference input to integrator $Z_1$)
    *   `X_1 ----> ∅; [MAK(1.0, u_2)]` (Disturbance acting on output $X_1$)
*   **Opportunist's Reactions:**
    1.  `X_1 ----> Z_2` (Feedback from output $X_1$ to integrator $Z_2$)
    2.  `Z_1 + Z_2 ----> ∅` (Antithetic annihilation, core of integral action)
    3.  `Z_1 ----> X_1 + Z_1` (Control action: $Z_1$ catalytically produces $X_1$)
    4.  `X_1 + X_1 ----> ∅` (Bimolecular degradation of $X_1$)
    5.  `∅ ----> X_1` (Basal production of $X_1$)

**Steady-State Analysis:**
Let $k_{u1}=1.0$ and $k_{u2}=1.0$ for the template reactions, and $k_1, k_2, k_3, k_4, k_5$ for the Opportunist's reactions.
The steady-state equations for the integrator species $Z_1$ and $Z_2$ are:
1.  $dZ_1/dt = k_{u1} u_1 - k_2 Z_1 Z_2 = 0 \implies k_{u1} u_1 = k_2 Z_1 Z_2$
2.  $dZ_2/dt = k_1 X_1 - k_2 Z_1 Z_2 = 0 \implies k_1 X_1 = k_2 Z_1 Z_2$

Equating the right-hand sides of (1) and (2) yields the integral control condition:
$k_{u1} u_1 = k_1 X_{1,ss}$
Given $k_{u1}=1.0$, for perfect adaptation ($X_{1,ss} = u_1$), the rate constant $k_1$ for `X_1 ----> Z_2` must be $1.0$. This establishes the tracking goal, independent of $u_2$.

Now, consider the steady-state equation for the output species $X_1$:
3.  $dX_1/dt = k_3 Z_1 + k_5 - k_4 X_1^2 - k_{u2} u_2 X_1 = 0$

Substituting $X_{1,ss} = u_1$ (from the integral action) into (3):
$k_3 Z_{1,ss} + k_5 - k_4 u_1^2 - k_{u2} u_2 u_1 = 0$

This equation must hold for any value of $u_2$. This is achieved by the integral controller adjusting the steady-state concentration of $Z_1$ (and consequently $Z_2$). We can solve for $Z_{1,ss}$:
$Z_{1,ss} = \frac{k_4 u_1^2 + k_{u2} u_2 u_1 - k_5}{k_3}$

This shows that $Z_{1,ss}$ (and thus $Z_{2,ss}$ via $Z_{1,ss} Z_{2,ss} = k_{u1} u_1 / k_2$) will adjust its concentration in response to the disturbance $u_2$ to maintain $X_{1,ss} = u_1$. This is precisely how an integral controller achieves robustness.

**Conclusion:** The Opportunist's design correctly implements the principles of antithetic integral control and achieves Robust Perfect Adaptation. The reactions are physically plausible mass action kinetics.

### Critique of the Contrarian's Proposal

The Contrarian's proposal, while aiming for radical alternatives, introduces a high degree of non-linearity that is likely to compromise robustness and stability, rather than enhance it.

**Control Theory and Physics Critique:**
The use of multiple quadratic terms for production, degradation, and interaction (e.g., `X_1 + X_1 ----> Z_2 + Z_2`, `Z_1 + Z_1 ----> ∅`, `Z_2 + Z_2 ----> X_1 + Z_2`, `Z_1 + Z_2 ----> Z_1 + Z_1`) makes the system analytically intractable for robust RPA and introduces significant challenges for practical implementation.
*   **Non-linear Integrator Dynamics:** The proposed integrator dynamics ($dZ_1/dt = k_{u1} u_1 - k_2 Z_1^2 + k_5 Z_1 Z_2$ and $dZ_2/dt = k_1 X_1^2 - k_3 Z_2^2 + k_5 Z_1 Z_2$) do not readily yield the simple $u_1 \propto X_1$ relationship that is characteristic of robust integral control. The non-linear terms (e.g., $Z_1^2$ degradation, $X_1^2$ production of $Z_2$) can lead to complex behaviors such as bistability, oscillations, or sensitivity to initial conditions, which are antithetical to robust performance.
*   **Problematic Interactions:** The reaction `Z_1 + Z_2 ----> Z_1 + Z_1` is particularly concerning. It implies that the interaction between $Z_1$ and $Z_2$ leads to the autocatalytic amplification of $Z_1$ while consuming $Z_2$. This can destabilize the antithetic balance, potentially leading to runaway concentrations of $Z_1$ or complete depletion of $Z_2$, thereby breaking the integral control mechanism.
*   **Lack of Theoretical Justification:** While non-linear control exists, these specific non-linearities are introduced without a clear theoretical framework for how they would guarantee RPA or improve stability. In the context of biochemical systems, simpler, more linear motifs are generally preferred for robust control due to their predictable behavior and easier analysis.

**Conclusion:** The Contrarian's design, while "radical," is unlikely to achieve Robust Perfect Adaptation due to its excessive and unprincipled non-linearities. These complexities introduce significant risks of instability and make the system's robust behavior highly improbable without extremely fine-tuned and non-robust parameter choices.

### SKEPTIC's Solution: A Canonical Antithetic Integral Controller

Based on established literature in biochemical control theory (e.g., Briat et al., 2016; Oishi & Klavins, 2011), the most robust and well-understood motif for achieving RPA via integral control is the antithetic integral controller (AIC). My proposed solution adheres to this canonical structure, ensuring both tracking and robustness.

**Template Reactions (Provided):**
1.  `∅ ----> Z_1; [MAK(1.0, u_1)]`
2.  `X_1 ----> ∅; [MAK(1.0, u_2)]`

**SKEPTIC's 5 Additional Reactions:**

1.  **`X_1 ----> Z_2`**
    *   **Reasoning:** This reaction establishes the feedback loop from the output species $X_1$ to the second integrator species $Z_2$. This is crucial for the controller to sense the output and drive the error to zero.
    *   **Type:** Unimolecular production.

2.  **`Z_1 + Z_2 ----> ∅`**
    *   **Reasoning:** This is the core antithetic annihilation reaction. It ensures that at steady state, the production rates of $Z_1$ (from $u_1$) and $Z_2$ (from $X_1$) are balanced, which is the fundamental mechanism for integral control and RPA.
    *   **Type:** Bimolecular degradation.

3.  **`Z_1 ----> X_1 + Z_1`**
    *   **Reasoning:** This reaction represents the control action. The integrator species $Z_1$ catalytically promotes the production of the output species $X_1$. This closes the control loop, allowing the integrator to influence the output.
    *   **Type:** Catalytic production.

4.  **`X_1 ----> ∅`**
    *   **Reasoning:** This reaction introduces a constitutive, unimolecular degradation of the output species $X_1$. This is a common component in control systems to ensure stability and prevent unbounded accumulation of the output.
    *   **Type:** Unimolecular degradation.

5.  **`∅ ----> X_1`**
    *   **Reasoning:** This reaction provides a basal, constitutive production of the output species $X_1$. This can help maintain a non-zero output even at low reference inputs and contributes to overall system stability.
    *   **Type:** Unimolecular synthesis.

**Steady-State Analysis for RPA:**

Let $k_{u1}=1.0$ and $k_{u2}=1.0$ be the rate constants for the template reactions.
Let $k_1, k_2, k_3, k_4, k_5$ be the rate constants for the proposed 5 reactions.

The differential equations for the species are:
*   $dZ_1/dt = k_{u1} u_1 - k_2 Z_1 Z_2$
*   $dZ_2/dt = k_1 X_1 - k_2 Z_1 Z_2$
*   $dX_1/dt = k_3 Z_1 + k_5 - k_4 X_1 - k_{u2} u_2 X_1$

At steady state ($d/dt = 0$ for all species):

1.  From $dZ_1/dt = 0$: $k_{u1} u_1 = k_2 Z_{1,ss} Z_{2,ss}$
2.  From $dZ_2/dt = 0$: $k_1 X_{1,ss} = k_2 Z_{1,ss} Z_{2,ss}$

Equating the right-hand sides of (1) and (2) yields the core integral control property:
$k_{u1} u_1 = k_1 X_{1,ss}$

Since $k_{u1}=1.0$ (from the template reaction), for the output $X_1$ to perfectly track the reference $u_1$ ($X_{1,ss} = u_1$), the rate constant $k_1$ for the reaction `X_1 ----> Z_2` must be set to $1.0$.
Thus, at steady state, **$X_{1,ss} = u_1$**. This confirms that the system achieves the tracking goal.

Now, let's verify robustness to the disturbance $u_2$. The steady-state value of $X_1$ is $u_1$, which is explicitly independent of $u_2$. The integral controller achieves this by dynamically adjusting the concentrations of its internal species.
From $dX_1/dt = 0$:
$k_3 Z_{1,ss} + k_5 - k_4 X_{1,ss} - k_{u2} u_2 X_{1,ss} = 0$

Substitute $X_{1,ss} = u_1$ into this equation:
$k_3 Z_{1,ss} + k_5 - k_4 u_1 - k_{u2} u_2 u_1 = 0$

We can solve for $Z_{1,ss}$:
$Z_{1,ss} = \frac{k_4 u_1 + k_{u2} u_2 u_1 - k_5}{k_3}$

This equation clearly shows that the steady-state concentration of $Z_1$ *depends on the disturbance $u_2$*. As $u_2$ changes, $Z_{1,ss}$ adjusts its value. This adjustment in $Z_{1,ss}$ (and consequently $Z_{2,ss}$ via the constant product $Z_{1,ss} Z_{2,ss}$) ensures that the production rate of $X_1$ (driven by $Z_1$) precisely balances the total degradation rate of $X_1$ (constitutive plus disturbance-induced), thereby maintaining $X_{1,ss} = u_1$ regardless of $u_2$. This confirms Robust Perfect Adaptation.

This design is a direct application of the canonical antithetic integral controller, which is a well-established and robust mechanism for achieving RPA in biochemical systems. All reactions are standard mass action kinetics and are physically plausible.

**Final List of 5 Reactions:**

2.  `Z_1 + Z_2 ----> ∅`
3.  `Z_1 ----> X_1 + Z_1`
4.  `X_1 ----> ∅`
5.  `∅ ----> X_1`
